Who we are

Basic Principles & Worldview

Every person is a whole world — and so, life at Kishorit is unique to every member.

Kishorit's members live in apartments in the village, work in its various branches, and enjoy health and leisure services. Some choose to live in nearby towns or to work outside Kishorit — but all are part of the community and its services.

"From each according to their ability, to each according to their needs" — this motto is the heart of life at Kishorit: a commitment to each member's changing needs, alongside the values of community, sharing and mutual responsibility.

Kishorit's members are not "wards" and not "patients" — they are members of an open, supportive community. They choose for themselves how to run their lives, accompanied by staff and family. Our members' free right to choose, in every area of life, guides everything we do.

The world of work

Kishorit sees employment as a significant layer of independence. 95% of members choose to work, even though they do not depend on it financially, and about 10% work outside Kishorit in businesses across the region.

  • Agriculture — organic vegetable garden, vineyard and winery, goat dairy and chicken coop
  • Crafts and industry — recycling and plastics
  • Services — laundry, kitchen and dining hall, maintenance and transportation
  • Media — the Kishorit TV studio

Agricultural work is especially beneficial: contact with nature and caring for animals develop curiosity, sharpen alertness and give a sense of empowerment.

Community life

At Kishorit, "community" carries its original meaning: a rich, supportive web of relationships. Regular gatherings in the shared dining hall, at work, in the gym and across the village create meaningful bonds between members.

Housing — a dynamic continuum

  • Private apartments — studios, shared apartments or living with a partner
  • Satellite apartments in Karmiel
  • Homes with close support
  • Nursing home — "Beit Shikma"

Every apartment is fully furnished with a refrigerator, television, telephone and an alert system — and each has its own private balcony.

The integrated village of Kishor

The village applies the principle of "normalization" — people with special needs and "ordinary" people living side by side — and brings together three populations:

Kishorit

  • A village for adults with special needs — 185 members.

Kishor

  • A renewing kibbutz for families, living alongside Kishorit in full cooperation.

Al-Fanara

  • A planned village for some 140 Arabic-speaking members with special needs, with an Arab staff and its own cultural character — out of a belief in the right of people of all faiths to community life.
