Body and soul

Health & Therapy

Kishorit's approach to health is holistic — seeing each person as a unity of body and soul, with personal choice at the center.

Our therapeutic approach

The principle of normalization

The principle of normalization recognizes the basic right of every person, including people with special needs, to live a full life in the community and to make decisions in every area of their life. This approach is anchored in law and is today's leading practice worldwide.

From automatic integration — to integration by choice

Traditional "integration" encouraged people with special needs to live among "ordinary" people, but at times produced loneliness and rejection. At Kishorit, integration happens by choice: people with special needs are the majority — and therefore the norm.

"Kishorit's members are equal and welcome partners in the community in which they live — a community that includes people with a wide range of abilities."

A continuum of rehabilitation services

We believe that a person's quality of life is not measured only by fixed criteria dictated from above, but also — and mainly — through a personal, ever-renewing process of defining wishes, needs, goals and aspirations.

There is no single, general rehabilitation program at Kishorit. Instead, there are personal, dynamic rehabilitation programs, led by each member's own choices.

Clinic and medical care

Kishorit provides medical services 24 hours a day, 7 days a week: a nursing team, a family physician, a psychiatrist and dental care. Members traveling to treatments outside the village are always accompanied by a staff member or volunteer.

Medical care is combined with proper nutrition, physical activity and emotional support — a whole-person approach.

The health house — "Beit Shikma"

Beit Shikma is dedicated to three groups of members:

Senior members

  • Members experiencing accelerated aging continue to live with dignity inside the community, among their friends.

Members in mental-health crisis

  • Care within the house instead of external hospitalization — so the rehabilitation process is not interrupted and the member stays in familiar surroundings.

Members recovering from illness

  • Supervision and support in a warm, familiar environment, instead of coping alone in an apartment.
